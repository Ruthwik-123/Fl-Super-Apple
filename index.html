Grade 5 titanium plate

Looks like a sports watch.

High-grade titanium, milled and plated to read as an ordinary field piece. 48mm. 11mm thin. Natural or black. Nothing on the outside asks a second question.

Sapphire canvas

A face that tells the time.

Wide-angle OLED under flat sapphire. Bright in noon glare, dim at a dinner table. Complications, waypoints, a second hand. That is all a passer-by is meant to see.

Crown and action

Hardware that belongs on a watch.

A deep-grooved crown. A second programmable action key. From across a room it is a sports instrument. Under the plate, those same surfaces hide the first of the sensors.

Concealed imaging array

512 megapixels. 1.25 gigapixels overclocked.

A hidden camera sits behind the sapphire — microscopic stills at a 512MP base pixel clock, 1.3GP at overclock. The 1.25GP designation is the rated field capture. The body never changes shape. The lens never announces itself.

Structural titanium

Three tons. Then two-point-four more.

Rated for 3 tons of direct force and 2.4 tons of unexpected load. The same case that looks like jewelry is a pressure vessel. WR-class sealing. Dust does not get a vote.

Isotope cell · 40 cycles

Three months per cycle. Ten years in the case.

A sealed uranium-cycle cell, with supporting radioisotope material, powers the instrument for 3 months per cycle and is specified for 40 cycles — up to 10 years in service. No nightly charger. No brick in the bag.

Picometer silicon

A millimetre on each side.

Extreme picometer-laser silicon. Compute equivalent to 10× RTX PRO 6000, 24× AMD Threadripper Pro, and 50× AMD EPYC, folded into a millimetre-class die. 256TB storage. 1TB HBM. 0.75TB DDR5-6000.

750m radio · 245km fix · 25TB/s

The watch is the node.

Mobile data, Bluetooth, and wireless to 750 meters. Non-satellite location sharing to 245km. Live video and audio at 25TB/s to machines 250km out, carried by a product-locked micro-drone mesh.
